If your coffee tastes flat or bitter, the grinder is usually to blame, not the beans or the machine. Ground coffee goes stale within minutes, and a cheap blade grinder smashes beans into a mix of dust and chunks that brew unevenly. A burr grinder crushes beans between two plates set a fixed distance apart, so every particle comes out close to the same size. That one change does more for your cup than almost anything else you can buy.

What to look for

  • Burrs, not blades. Conical or flat both work well at home.
  • Enough grind settings for your brew method. Drip and pour-over need medium, French press needs coarse, espresso needs very fine.
  • Easy cleaning. Removable hoppers and burrs make a monthly clean painless.
  • Low retention, meaning little old coffee stays trapped inside to mix with today's grounds.

Which grind size for which brewer?

  • French press and cold brew: coarse, like sea salt.
  • Drip coffee maker: medium, like sand.
  • Pour-over (V60, Chemex): medium-fine.
  • Espresso: fine, like powdered sugar. Most budget grinders struggle here.

Keep it working

  • Grind only what you need right before brewing.
  • Brush out the burrs and chute every few weeks.
  • Never wash burrs with water unless the manual says you can. Use a brush or grinder-cleaning tablets.
  • Keep beans in an airtight container, not in the hopper for days.
