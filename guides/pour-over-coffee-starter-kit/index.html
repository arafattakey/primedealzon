Pour-over coffee is clean, bright and easy to fine-tune, and the gear is cheaper than most coffee makers. You need three things: a dripper, the right paper filters and a kettle that pours slowly. Add a burr grinder and you have the same setup many specialty cafés use.

1. The dripper: Hario V60

The V60 is the most popular pour-over cone in the world for good reason. Its spiral ridges and single large hole let the speed of your pour control the brew. Pour slowly for a stronger cup or faster for a lighter one. The ceramic version is made in Japan from traditional Arita-yaki porcelain and holds heat well. Size 02 makes one to four cups.

2. The filters

V60 cones need V60-shaped filters. Regular flat-bottom filters won't fit. Buy the size that matches your dripper (02 for both drippers above), and rinse each filter with hot water before adding coffee so it doesn't add a papery taste.

3. The kettle: why a gooseneck matters

A regular kettle dumps water in a splash. A gooseneck spout lets you pour a thin, steady stream exactly where you want it, which is the whole trick of pour-over. A simple V60 recipe

  • 15 g coffee (about 2.5 tablespoons), ground medium-fine, to 250 g water (about 8.5 oz).
  • Rinse the filter, add the coffee and level the bed.
  • Bloom: pour about 30 g of water, just enough to wet the grounds, and wait 30–45 seconds.
  • Pour the rest in slow circles over 1.5 minutes, avoiding the filter walls.
  • Total brew time should be about 3 minutes. Too slow means grind coarser. Too fast means grind finer.
