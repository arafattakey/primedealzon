If they already own an espresso machine

Accessories are the safe choice here, because they work with any machine.

A scale is the upgrade that changes the most. Espresso recipes are ratios, such as 18 g of coffee in and 36 g of espresso out in about 25 to 30 seconds. Without a scale, every shot is a guess. The Greater Goods scale reads to 0.1 g and has a built-in timer. Check that it fits on the machine's drip tray under the cup, since some compact machines have little room there.

For anyone who drinks espresso: Bodum Pavina glasses

A pair of 2.5 oz double-wall glasses in borosilicate glass. The air gap keeps the shot warm and the outside cool, and you can see the crema and layers through the glass. They work for anyone who drinks espresso, whatever they brew it with.

They are delicate. Breakage is the most common complaint, with owners reporting cracks from small knocks, and the narrow base can tip. Hand washing is the gentler choice. Think of them as the nice glasses, not the everyday ones.

For the latte drinker: Zulay milk frother

A battery-powered wand that whips warm milk into foam in seconds. It won't match the microfoam from a steam wand, but for someone with a moka pot or a pod machine it's the cheapest way to make a latte-style drink at home. It doesn't heat the milk, so that happens first on the stove or in the microwave. It needs two AA batteries.

No machine yet: Bialetti Moka Express

The moka pot is how millions of Italian homes make coffee. Water in the base boils and pushes up through the grounds, producing a small, strong cup. The 3-cup size makes about one mug's worth. It's made in Italy, works on gas and electric stoves, and looks good left out on the stovetop.

Be clear about what it is. A moka pot brews at much lower pressure than an espresso machine, so the coffee is strong and rich but has little or no crema. Most people who like espresso enjoy it anyway. The classic aluminum version doesn't work on induction without an adapter plate.

For the traveler: Wacaco Nanopresso

A hand-pumped espresso maker about the size of a water bottle. Add fine ground coffee and hot water, pump, and it produces a real shot with no battery or power. Owners who camp or travel for work like having espresso anywhere.

It takes patience. Owners report leaks when the grind isn't right, and it has several small parts to rinse and dry after each use. Some also report cracked parts within a year of heavy use. Give it to someone who enjoys tinkering, and skip it for someone who wants a quick cup.

The big gift: Breville Bambino

The Bambino is a compact machine built for people starting out. According to the listing it heats up in seconds, holds water temperature with digital control and wets the coffee gently before full pressure. The steam wand froths milk automatically, which removes the hardest skill for a beginner.

Owners like the small footprint and the quality of the espresso for the money. There are two things to plan for. It has no grinder, and espresso needs fresh, fine, even grounds, so it only makes sense for someone who has a capable burr grinder or will get one. And some owners report steam wand problems or shots that aren't hot enough, with mixed experiences getting repairs. Keep the box and the receipt.

Gift checklist

  • They own a machine: scale, knock box or glasses.
  • They don't own a machine: moka pot for a small gift, Bambino for a big one.
  • Buying the Bambino? Check that they have an espresso-capable grinder.
  • Add a bag of freshly roasted espresso beans, with a roast date in the last two or three weeks.
  • Order early. Espresso machines sell out and shipping slows in December.

Tip: Not sure what they already own? The Pavina glasses and a bag of good beans work for every espresso drinker, with or without a machine.

What do you buy someone who loves espresso?

If they have a machine, a small accessory they use daily: a 0.1 g scale, a knock box or double-wall espresso glasses. If they don't, a moka pot is a low-cost start and a compact machine like the Breville Bambino is the larger gift.

Is an espresso machine a good gift?

It can be, for someone who has said they want one and has counter space. Remember the grinder: espresso needs fresh, fine grounds, so a machine without a suitable grinder often ends up unused.

Does a moka pot make real espresso?

No. It brews at far lower pressure than an espresso machine, so the result is strong, concentrated coffee with little or no crema. It works well in milk drinks and many espresso fans enjoy it.

What is a good small gift for a home barista?

A scale that reads to 0.1 g, a knock box, or espresso glasses. All three are useful with any machine and are things people often put off buying for themselves.
